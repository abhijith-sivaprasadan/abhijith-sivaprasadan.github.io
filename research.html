Research lens · /research

Rocket cooling-channel heat transfer, surface condition and measurable degradation.

My thesis at Siemens Energy Finspång was about turning CFD/CHT evidence into test-rig decisions at high temperature, with limited experimental access. The hardest problems in thermal-fluid engineering sit at the boundary between simulation assumptions and physical hardware behaviour.

My interest is in predicting, detecting and interpreting thermally driven degradation in high-temperature flow systems, especially where deposit formation or a change in surface condition alters heat transfer in ways a steady-state model does not capture. I am most motivated by rocket cooling-channel problems, where experiments, models and monitoring indicators have to work together to support reusable hardware.

The bridge, drawn

Same question in two systems: which resistance dominates?

In the Pulsatorn reducer, Bi = 0.003–0.004 showed that through-wall gradients were negligible next to the fluid-side and external pathways. In a methane-cooled channel, a deposit adds a new resistance at the wall. Decompose first, then decide what to measure.

Open questions

Six questions, each with the measurement that would move it

Q1
Conjugate heat transfer in compressible, high-temperature passages
NEXT BOUNDARY · PROPOSED
A small heated-channel rig, Re 10⁴ to 10⁵, with embedded thermocouples. Compare measured heat-transfer coefficients with Bartz-scaled predictions and k-omega SST results.
Q2
Thermal resistance decomposition
NEXT BOUNDARY
Report every CHT result with its resistance split, so a temperature field can be traced to a mechanism.
Q3
Experimental validation under high-temperature constraints
NEXT BOUNDARY
Measurement-chain design with uncertainty tracked from sensor to result, for rigs where hardware access is limited.
Q4
Deposit formation and its heat-transfer consequences
NEXT BOUNDARY
Model structures able to separate deposit growth from boundary-condition error, in the catalytic-pyrolysis and coking setting.
Q5
Predictive monitoring indicators for thermally loaded hardware
NEXT BOUNDARY
Indicators derived from coupled simulation and measurement data, not from isolated threshold rules.
Q6
Numerical methods for thermal-fluid systems
NEXT BOUNDARY
Mesh independence, near-wall treatment and turbulence-model sensitivity reported as one convergence discipline.
